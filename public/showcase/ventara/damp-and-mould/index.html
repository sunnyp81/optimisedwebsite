Damp, mould and ventilation

Damp affects 1 in 20 homes in England. Ventilation lets moisture leave the building.

Why ventilation matters

Damp is common in English homes and the rules for landlords are tighter. Every figure here links to its source.

  • 1.4mhomes

    in England had a damp problem in 2024. That is 5% of all homes.

    Source 1: Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government↗ (opens in a new tab)

  • 10%of private rented homes

    had a damp problem in 2024, against 4% of owner occupied homes.

    Source 1: Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government↗ (opens in a new tab)

  • 10working days

    From 27 October 2025, social landlords must investigate a potential significant damp and mould hazard within 10 working days of becoming aware of it, under Awaab's Law.

    Source 2: GOV.UK↗ (opens in a new tab)

  • 24hours

    Social landlords must investigate an emergency hazard and make it safe within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, under Awaab's Law.

    Source 2: GOV.UK↗ (opens in a new tab)

“If a dwelling is inadequately ventilated, moisture in the air cannot escape the building and is therefore more likely to cause condensation.”

UK Health Security Agency guidance on damp and mould. Source 3↗ (opens in a new tab)

The same guidance says damp and mould primarily affect the airways and lungs, and can also affect the eyes and skin. People with certain health conditions, children and older adults are at greater risk of more severe health impacts. Ventilation in dwellings in England is covered by Approved Document F, Volume 1↗ (opens in a new tab) of the Building Regulations, in force from 15 June 2022.
